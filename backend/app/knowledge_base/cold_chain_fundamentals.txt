1. WHAT IS COLD-CHAIN LOGISTICS?
==================================================

Cold-chain logistics is the process of storing, handling and transporting
temperature-sensitive products under specified environmental conditions.

The objective is to maintain the product's required temperature range
throughout its journey, from origin to destination.

Common temperature-sensitive products include:
- Fresh fruits and vegetables
- Dairy products
- Frozen foods
- Pharmaceuticals
- Biological materials

The acceptable temperature range depends on the specific product.
Never assume that one temperature range applies to every shipment.

==================================================
2. TEMPERATURE MONITORING
==================================================

Temperature monitoring helps operators identify deviations from the
required shipment temperature.

Important values:
- Current temperature: Latest recorded shipment temperature.
- Required temperature: Product-specific target or permitted range.
- Temperature deviation: Difference between the recorded temperature
  and the applicable target or permitted limit.

A temperature deviation should trigger investigation.

Recommended investigation steps:
1. Verify the recorded reading and its timestamp.
2. Compare it with the shipment's required temperature range.
3. Check refrigeration equipment and power supply.
4. Review available historical temperature readings.
5. Record the incident and notify the responsible operator.
6. Follow the applicable product-specific SOP.

A single reading does not always establish product damage or spoilage.
Product disposition should be decided using validated procedures and
qualified personnel.

==================================================
3. BATTERY AND POWER MONITORING
==================================================

Battery status can affect refrigeration equipment and monitoring devices.

Operators should consider:
- Current battery percentage.
- Battery discharge trend.
- Availability of an external power supply.
- Refrigeration equipment operating status.
- Estimated remaining operating time, if reliably available.

A low battery reading should prompt an equipment and power-supply check.

Do not assume that a particular battery percentage guarantees a specific
remaining refrigeration duration. Actual runtime depends on equipment,
battery capacity, load and operating conditions.

==================================================
4. SHIPMENT DELAYS
==================================================

Shipment delays can increase the time a product remains in transit.

Possible causes include:
- Traffic congestion.
- Vehicle breakdown.
- Weather-related disruption.
- Loading or unloading delays.
- Route restrictions.
- Hub or warehouse congestion.

Recommended actions:
1. Check the latest available shipment status.
2. Review the estimated arrival time and reported delay.
3. Assess the refrigeration and battery status.
4. Identify whether an alternate route or hub is feasible.
5. Inform the responsible operations team.
6. Record decisions and relevant timestamps.

An estimated delay should not be presented as a confirmed delivery time.

==================================================
5. TEMPERATURE EXCURSION RESPONSE
==================================================

A temperature excursion occurs when a recorded temperature moves outside
the applicable product-specific permitted range.

A general response workflow is:

1. DETECT
   Identify the temperature deviation.

2. VERIFY
   Check the reading, timestamp and available supporting records.

3. INVESTIGATE
   Inspect refrigeration, power supply, vehicle conditions and shipment
   handling information.

4. ESCALATE
   Notify the responsible operator or qualified personnel according
   to the applicable SOP.

5. DOCUMENT
   Record readings, observations, actions and decisions.

6. REVIEW
   Determine the next action using validated product-specific procedures.

Do not automatically declare a shipment safe or unsafe based only on
one temperature reading.

==================================================
6. IMPORTANT OPERATIONAL METRICS
==================================================

Temperature deviation:
Difference between the recorded temperature and the applicable target.

Battery level:
Reported remaining battery charge as a percentage.

Shipment delay:
Difference between the expected and updated delivery schedule.

Estimated time of arrival:
A calculated estimate based on available route and timing information.

Temperature trend:
A sequence of readings used to understand how temperature changes
over time.

These metrics should be interpreted together rather than in isolation.

7. LIMITATIONS AND SAFETY
==================================================

This document provides general educational guidance only.

It is not:
- An official company operating procedure.
- A substitute for product-specific storage requirements.
- A regulatory compliance document.
- A food-safety or pharmaceutical quality assessment.

CryoNexus AI must not invent missing telemetry, shipment events,
company policies or regulatory requirements.

When information is insufficient, the system should clearly explain
what information is missing.

For safety-critical decisions, users must follow validated product-specific
SOPs and consult qualified personnel.
